Miami Sunset Cruise Guide: Biscayne Bay and Millionaire's Row

The boat leaves at 6:30pm for a reason. Here is the route, where the light actually hits the skyline, and what to bring so you are not cold on the water.

Published 2026-10-12Reading time 4 minWritten by Leonida Tours

The reason to take a boat at sunset, rather than watch from the beach, is that the beach sees the sun set behind the city. From the water you watch the city light up instead, which is the better view and the less obvious one.

The route

A standard two-hour Miami sunset cruise leaves from the marina, runs out through the cut between Key Biscayne and Virginia Key, turns north up Biscayne Bay, and comes back along the shoreline with the skyline on your right. The loop passes, in order, the PortMiami cranes and the cruise ships, Fisher Island and the mansions, the Brickell towers, and the causeway as you come back in.

The whole point of the geometry is that you see Miami in two passes. Outbound, the towers are ahead of you and the water is empty. Return, the towers are beside you and lit up, and the port is on your other side. Most people only ever see one of those.

When the light actually happens

Time in NovemberWhat you see
5:45pmGolden light on the water, the skyline still reading as silhouettes
6:00pmSun low over the western bay, towers starting to catch colour
6:15pmSunset proper. The water turns copper, the glass goes orange
6:25pmBlue hour. The skyline lights come on and the buildings turn blue-grey
6:40pm onwardsFull night, lit skyline, and the city reflecting in the bay

Those times shift by an hour either way across the year. In June the sunset is around 8:10pm and the whole sequence moves later. Check the actual sunset time for the date you are travelling, and aim to be on the water 45 minutes before it.

Millionaire's Row

Along the southern tip of Miami Beach, facing the bay, is a strip of about forty waterfront mansions on Indian Creek. It is the most expensive real estate in Florida by a distance, and from the water it is a wall of architecture with no public access behind it.

The buildings you can actually identify are the white, blocky Deco-modern one at 1000 Ocean Drive, the Cuban Memorial Boulevard scale of the harbour beyond it, and the low green bulk of the golf course on the island behind. Seeing it from a boat is the only sensible way, and it is one of the more surreal stretches of urban geography in the United States.

What to bring

  • A light layer. It is on the water, and even in November the wind off Biscayne Bay drops the temperature noticeably after sunset. A windbreaker is more useful than a coat.
  • Sunscreen. You will be out for two hours with the sun on you the whole way.
  • Your own water. It is on the boat, but in a cup that you will spill.
  • Flat shoes. The boarding ladder into the boat is a step down and a step back up, and the deck is wet after 8pm.
  • A phone with a clip or a strap. The light is genuinely good and the deck moves.

Boat or convertible

A fair question, because both show you the skyline at sunset. They are not the same thing at all.

What you seeThe city from the water, plus the skyline from the bayThe same skyline, from the road
Port and islandsYou pass the cranes and Fisher IslandYou drive past them at 20 mph
PhotosWater, horizon, reflections, bow shotsNeon facades, close-up, palm trees
ComfortWindy, cooler, spray on the bowStill, warm, traffic

Best forThe bay, the sunset and the skyline lit upThe Art Deco strip and the murals

If you can only do one, do the boat. The skyline from the water is the image that stays with people, and the waterfront is the part of Miami that you cannot get anywhere else.

Boarding, and what the crew actually does

Boarding is at a floating dock, so there is a step ladder and a crew member counting heads. The boat is a proper passenger vessel with shade, seating, a bathroom and a crew, not a pontoon with a speaker. The music is on, there is a drink, and the crew will point out the landmarks as you pass them.

The one thing worth knowing: the boat goes out at 6:30pm regardless of the sunset time, so in summer you are on the water long before the light and back after dark, and in December you are on the water in the last of the golden hour. Either way it works, because the route takes you past the skyline twice.

Weather

Boats do not run in bad weather, and on a stormy afternoon the marina will cancel. If you are on a fixed schedule, check the weather the morning of and keep a dry plan for the evening.

Frequently asked questions

What time is the Miami sunset cruise?

Our departure is 6:30pm, which puts you on the water for golden hour and puts you back at the marina around 8:30pm. Sunset in Miami in November is around 5:30pm; in June it is around 8:10pm.

How long is the sunset cruise?

Two hours, with the boat out on Biscayne Bay for roughly 90 of them. It is a loop out through the cut and back along the shoreline with the skyline lit up.

What can you see from a Miami sunset boat?

PortMiami's cranes and cruise ships, Fisher Island, the mansions of Millionaire's Row, the Brickell and Downtown skyline, and the causeway on the way back in. You see the city twice, from two angles.

Is the sunset cruise worth it?

Yes, if you want the skyline from the water rather than from the road. A convertible gives you the Art Deco strip and the murals; the boat gives you the bay, the islands and the reflections.

What should I wear on a Miami boat?

Light layers and flat shoes. It is windy on the water and cooler after sunset than on land, the deck gets wet, and you board down a step ladder.
